
## **Comprehensive Clinical Study Report: Anterior Open Bite Closure**

### ​**1. Study Overview & Methodology (Original Protocol)**

- ​**Trial Title:** Anterior open bite closure by clear aligners versus fixed appliances in adult patients with skeletal open bite: A randomized clinical trial.

- ​**Design:** A 2-arm parallel randomized clinical trial with a 1:1 allocation ratio.

- ​**Setting:** Faculty of Dentistry, Cairo University.

- ​**Sample Size:** 20 adult subjects (10 per group).

- ​**Inclusion Criteria:**
    
    - ​Adult patients aged 17–25 years.
    
    - ​Skeletal open bite (SNMP > 37, MMA > 28) and dental open bite of 3–8 mm.
    
    - ​Normal incisal show and minimal crowding.

        ### ​**2. Anchorage and Mechanics (As Planned)**
        
        - ​**TAD Specifications:** Four mini-screws (10 mm x 1.6 mm) per patient.
        
        - ​**Placement:** Two infrazygomatic screws and two screws placed palatally between the upper second premolar and first molar.
        
        - ​**Force Delivery:** Memory chains extended from mini-screws to bonded buttons (Aligner Group) or hooks on a 19x25 SS wire (Fixed Group).
        
        ### ​**3. Clinical Findings & Protocol Deviations (Aligner Group)**
        
        - ​**Initial Setup:** Alternated box attachments (one side) and buttons (other side) tooth-by-tooth for stability and anchorage.
        
        - ​**Tracking Mismatch:** The programmed intrusion of 0.25 mm/month (3 mm total over 6 months) failed to track clinically within the first two months.
        
        - ​**Movement Unpredictability:** Intrusion was inconsistent, with upper second molars often over-intruded while first molars under-intruded.
        - ​**Workflow Modifications:**
            - ​**Abolished Programming:** Pre-programmed software intrusion was discontinued due to the tracking mismatch.
            - ​**2-Week Delivery:** The protocol was updated to include scans and aligner delivery every two weeks to manage tracking loss.
            - ​**Reactive Reprogramming:** To counter material flexibility and buccal displacement, subsequent aligners were programmed with 3° lingual root torque.
            - ​**Mechanical Adjustments:** Manually swapped positions of attachments, buttons, and memory chains several times to correct displacement.
        - ​**Finishing Stage:** To resolve premature canine contacts preventing full closure, the upper canines were programmed for 3° of expansion and 6°–9° of torque across three aligners.
        
        ### ​**4. Segmented Fixed Appliance Group: Findings & Complications**
        
        - ​**Approach:** Brackets and wires (segmented) from canine to second molar.
        
        - ​**Consolidation:** This group demonstrated superior arch consolidation compared to the aligner group.
        - ​**Occlusal Plane Issues:**
            - ​The segmented approach resulted in an uneven occlusal plane.
            - ​The anterior teeth in the segment (canines and first premolars) showed the least amount of intrusion.
        - ​**Elevated TAD Failure:** This group experienced a higher rate of TAD failure compared to the aligner group, primarily due to increased food stagnation around the braces and poor oral hygiene.
        
        ### ​**5. Comparative Summary and Conclusion**
        
        - ​**Intrusion Speed:** Clinical observation showed that intrusion was **faster** in the aligner group compared to the fixed appliance group.
        - ​**Shared Complications:**
            - ​Consistent TAD migration and failures in both groups, heavily linked to hygiene.
            - ​High rate of attachment failure under intrusive loading in the aligner group.
            - ​Premature canine contacts hindered final bite closure in both groups, requiring specific corrective programming.
        Interventions and Data Analysis

Preintrusion phase

All patients were treated with a segmented fixed appliances (MBT prescription, 0.022 × 0.028-inch slot, Gemini brackets; 3M Unitek, St. Paul, MN) bonded bilaterally on the maxillary segments extending from the canines to the second molars. The leveling and alignment phase was carried out until a 0.019 × 0.025-inch stainless steel segmented archwire was in place.

Intrusion phase

For each patient, four miniscrews (10 × 1.6 mm; 3M Unitek TADs) were inserted: two infrazygomatic screws and two palatal screws placed between the first and second molars. The palatal screws were positioned 8–10 mm from the gingival margin and inserted perpendicular to the vertical palatal shelf. After two weeks, miniscrew stability was verified, and pre-intrusion cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) scans were obtained (i-CAT CBCT unit; Imaging Sciences International, Hatfield, PA) to establish baseline measurements (T1). Upper and lower working models were prepared to fabricate palatal wires for intrusion and a passive lingual holding arch for the mandibular arch. Intrusion was performed for a period of 6 months using interrupted intrusive forces delivered by SUPER Elasto-Force plastic chains (Dentaurum, Ispringen, Germany). Each miniscrew was connected to corresponding crimpable hooks buccally and palatally with two elastic memory chains. A net intrusive force of 400 g per side (approximately 40 g per root) was applied to the maxillary segments. Force magnitude and direction were confirmed by analyzing pre-intrusion CBCT data to determine the resultant force vectors. To reinforce anchorage, the mandibular posterior segment was stabilized with a 0.019 × 0.025-inch stainless steel segmented archwire and a passive lingual holding arch. Patients were recalled every 2 weeks to assess appliance integrity, evaluate miniscrew stability, and replace the elastic memory chains. At each visit, anterior open bite was measured clinically using a digital caliper (VINCA DCLA-0605). After 6 months, post-intrusion CBCT scans (T2) were obtained to evaluate the amount of maxillary molar intrusion achieved.
